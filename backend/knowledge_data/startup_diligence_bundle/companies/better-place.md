# Better Place

**Category:** Startup Due Diligence Case Study  
**Outcome:** `FAILED`  
**Source Document:** `startup_failures_detailedv2.pdf` (Pages 4)

## Executive Summary
Electric-vehicle infrastructure startup that built battery-swapping stations, raising roughly 850 million dollars before its 2013 bankruptcy.

## Business Model & Economics
Planned to sell electric cars with swappable batteries and build a network of battery-swap stations, requiring enormous upfront infrastructure capital.

## Failure Factors & Root Causes
Required simultaneous, capital-intensive buildout of infrastructure, vehicles, and consumer adoption all at once, and depended on automaker partners who ultimately did not commit to its battery-swap standard at scale.

## Risk Indicators Observed
A business plan requiring coordinated success across infrastructure buildout, hardware manufacturing partners, and consumer behavior change all at once.

## Due Diligence Lessons for Investors
Investors should be especially cautious of business models requiring several different massive capital commitments to succeed simultaneously, since any single failure point can collapse the whole plan.

## Key Takeaway
> Businesses requiring multiple simultaneous, capital-intensive bets to succeed are exceptionally fragile, since any one failed dependency can doom the entire venture.
