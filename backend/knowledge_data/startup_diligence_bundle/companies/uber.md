# Uber

**Category:** Startup Due Diligence Case Study  
**Outcome:** `SUCCESSFUL`  
**Source Document:** `startup_successes_detailed.pdf` (Pages 2)

## Executive Summary
Ride-hailing platform connecting riders with independent drivers through a mobile app.

## Business Model & Economics
Commission on each ride taken through the platform, later expanded into food delivery (Uber Eats) and freight.

## Success Factors & Competitive Moat
Solved real friction in urban transportation (hailing, payment, and pricing transparency) and expanded aggressively city by city ahead of regulatory clarity.

## Growth Drivers & Flywheels
Network effects between riders and drivers in each city, surge pricing that balanced supply and demand, and rapid international expansion.

## Investor Due Diligence Lessons
Aggressive geographic expansion can build a durable lead in networked marketplaces, but it also invites regulatory and labor-classification risk that due diligence must weigh.

## Key Takeaway
> Winning a two-sided marketplace often means tolerating short-term losses and regulatory friction to lock in long-term network density.
