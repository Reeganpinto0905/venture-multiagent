# Shyp

**Category:** Startup Due Diligence Case Study  
**Outcome:** `FAILED`  
**Source Document:** `startup_failures_detailedv2.pdf` (Pages 6, 7)

## Executive Summary
On-demand package-pickup and shipping startup that would collect, pack, and ship items for customers, before shutting down in 2018.

## Business Model & Economics
Charged a flat pickup fee plus shipping costs, but the labor-intensive pickup-and-pack process cost significantly more per transaction than the fee it charged.

## Failure Factors & Root Causes
Underlying unit economics never worked because the labor cost of individually picking up and packing each item exceeded what customers were willing to pay, and later attempts to pivot toward business customers came too late.

## Risk Indicators Observed
A per-transaction cost structure that is fundamentally higher than the price charged, with no clear path for scale to close that gap.

## Due Diligence Lessons for Investors
Investors should model whether a service's per-transaction labor cost can ever fall below its price point at any realistic scale, rather than assuming scale alone will fix negative unit economics.

## Key Takeaway
> Some labor-intensive service models have unit economics that scale cannot fix, since the core cost driver does not meaningfully decrease with volume.
