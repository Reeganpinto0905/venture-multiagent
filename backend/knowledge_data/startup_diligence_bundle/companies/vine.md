# Vine

**Category:** Startup Due Diligence Case Study  
**Outcome:** `FAILED`  
**Source Document:** `startup_failures_detailedv2.pdf` (Pages 3)

## Executive Summary
Short-form looping video-sharing app acquired by Twitter in 2012 and shut down in 2016 despite a large, engaged creator community.

## Business Model & Economics
Free consumer app with no direct monetization built for creators, relying on parent company Twitter to eventually build a revenue model around it.

## Failure Factors & Root Causes
Twitter never built a sustainable monetization or creator-payout system, so top creators migrated to platforms like Instagram and, later, TikTok's precursors that did pay creators.

## Risk Indicators Observed
A large, engaged user base with no monetization plan, and a parent company deprioritizing investment in the acquired product over time.

## Due Diligence Lessons for Investors
Investors should check whether an acquired product has a credible path to monetization under its new parent, not just a large user base.

## Key Takeaway
> User engagement without a credible monetization or creator-incentive plan is not enough to keep a platform's community loyal long-term.
